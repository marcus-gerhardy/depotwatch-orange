# Milestones and the year in review
Two ways of looking back at what you decided.

## Milestones {#ms-what}
The milestone catalogue records small markers: the first transaction, 100,000 sats, the first withdrawal from an exchange into your own custody, the first position past the holding period, a transfer history kept in order.

One rule decides what gets in: **a decision is rewarded, never the price.** There is nothing for the price going up, because that is not an achievement, it is weather. There are no streaks to lose either, and nothing is compared with anyone — the app has never seen another user.

Five categories: stacking, sovereignty, patience, diligence, culture.

## Reading the whitepaper {#ms-whitepaper}
Bitcoin in nine pages, by Satoshi Nakamoto: [Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System (PDF)](/bitcoin.pdf). The file ships with this app rather than sitting on somebody else's server — opening it asks nobody for permission and tells nobody about it.

That is the "whitepaper opened" milestone, from the culture category. It is recorded on the click; if that happens before a portfolio file is open, it is credited to the next file you open within a day.

## When something counts as reached {#ms-when}
The app evaluates on every change and once when a file is opened. What is reached is recorded with the date it **actually** happened — so a file with five years of history gets historical dates, not today's.

Once reached stays reached: if the holding later falls back below a mark, that changes nothing.

The first time a file without milestone history is opened it stays quiet — what it already fulfils was discovered, not reached.

## Year in review {#ms-year}
The year in review sums up a **completed** year in cards: net stacked, invested, your volume-weighted average price against the year's average, the range of prices paid, busiest month and weekday, longest buying streak, fees, what passed the holding period, what was realised, self custody, milestones, holding at year end.

The running year is not offered: an interim figure in March says something different from the same figure in December. The review is reachable from the button on the milestones page and from the dashboard widget.

## Sharing as an image {#ms-share}
The review can be saved as a PNG. The image is created **entirely in your browser**; nothing is uploaded.

Absolute amounts are **hidden by default** — holding, sums invested, sats. What remains is relative: counts, percentages, average price. Anyone who does want to show them switches that on explicitly; the app says that this makes the real size of your holding public, and that this is a security risk for you personally.

In privacy mode, absolute amounts cannot be written into an image at all.
